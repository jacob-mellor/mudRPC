# gRPC over LoRa and mesh radio

**gRPC over LoRa and mesh radio** is what a 512-byte sealed packet with no connection makes possible: gRPC's service model where the link is a radio that is down more than up, a mesh that stores and forwards, or a LoRa frame of a few hundred bytes.

A mesh radio delivers when it can. A message may wait in a relay for an hour and arrive after the sender has moved on; two messages may arrive in the wrong order, or twice. gRPC's connection cannot live there. mudRPC's peer was built for it: every call has a ttl, the longest it is worth delivering, a week at most, and the mesh may hold it that long; a reply that arrives after the ttl is dropped and the call reported expired; a duplicate is dropped by its nonce, and a replay, however late, by a window of the newest 64 counters per sender. The payload is bounded by the frame, the number [From gRPC](../README.md#from-grpc) gives, and the stubs encode it for you.

The transport is yours, since no two radios have one API: the peer hands you each outgoing item, a topic, a wire and its ttl, and you hand it each wire you receive; `sent()` tells it the radio took the item, and a radio that refuses keeps it for the next tick. The library's own `Udp`, step 5 of every skill, is the one transport shipped, and Reticulum's and DTN's adapters are their authors', written against [adapters/CONFORMANCE.md](../adapters/CONFORMANCE.md); the simulator, `sim`, is the mesh you test against before a radio exists, with partitions, jamming, bandwidth collapse, floods and replays as scenario lines, and the six harnesses print the same log byte for byte.

Topics are the mesh's addressing: a peer listens on its own name for its replies and on each service it serves, and a call goes out on the service's name: every server that hears it answers, the caller keeps the first reply and drops the rest, so a device is called by its own name and a fleet is told by a publish. Events, a publish with no reply, ride the same topics. Over a radio the fleet key seals every wire and a signed fleet proves each sender, so a relay in the middle learns nothing but sizes and timing, and forges nothing.

The one-line change for a gRPC client is in [From gRPC](../README.md#from-grpc). This page belongs to [gRPC without HTTP/2](grpc-without-http2.md); the [home page](../README.md) has the five-minute start.
